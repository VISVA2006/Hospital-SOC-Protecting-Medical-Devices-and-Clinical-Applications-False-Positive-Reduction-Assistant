# 1. Title
**Hospital Security Operations Center (SOC) Protecting Medical Devices and Clinical Applications: False-Positive Reduction Assistant with Novel-Threat Preservation**

---

# 2. Abstract
Modern healthcare institutions are high-value targets for cyberattacks, ranging from targeted ransomware to unauthorized clinical data exfiltration. Healthcare Security Operations Centers (SOCs) face an overwhelming volume of security alerts generated by clinical networks, medical Internet of Things (IoMT) devices, and hospital information systems. Over 70% of these alerts represent repetitive, benign false positives caused by authorized network scans, diagnostic bursts, and routine device maintenance. This leads to debilitating analyst fatigue, directly increasing the likelihood that subtle, life-critical cyber intrusions will be overlooked. 

This project presents the first 35% working milestone of an end-to-end, defensive False-Positive Reduction Assistant. Operating strictly on a realistic synthetic dataset of 5,330 clinical security alerts, the system integrates a stream-integrity processing layer, machine-learning classification, an Isolation Forest novelty-detection engine, deterministic clinical safety guardrails, and explainable AI (XAI) evidence generation. In controlled empirical experiments against a traditional rule-based SOC baseline, the proposed architecture reduced manual alert review workload by **44.01%** (saving **209.20 analyst hours** across 5,330 alerts at 8 minutes per review), improved threat triage precision from **34.53% to 61.67%**, and achieved a **0.00% missed incident rate** (zero false negatives on confirmed threats). Crucially, the system passed eight adversarial test suites, demonstrating robustness against duplicate alert floods, out-of-order event streams, delayed arrivals, and adversarial model deception attacks against critical medical devices.

# 3. Introduction
Healthcare infrastructure increasingly relies on interconnected medical devices, including infusion pumps, mechanical ventilators, physiological patient monitors, and radiological imaging systems (PACS/CT/MRI). While network connectivity enables centralized telemetry and rapid clinical intervention, it expands the hospital's cyber-physical attack surface. 

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems and Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) deployed in hospitals generate thousands of alerts daily. Because medical devices frequently rely on proprietary protocols, legacy operating systems (such as VxWorks or Windows Embedded), and unencrypted messaging standards (HL7/DICOM), conventional rule-based detection systems flag routine clinical transmissions as anomalous. SOC analysts are forced to spend disproportionate amounts of time triaging repetitive non-malicious alerts, inducing alert fatigue.

This research prototype introduces an intelligent, human-in-the-loop assistant that learns from historical analyst dispositions to safely suppress repetitive false positives while enforcing strict non-suppression guardrails whenever an alert involves critical patient-care equipment or novel behavioral patterns.

# 4. Problem Statement
Hospital SOC analysts spend the vast majority of their operational shift manually investigating repetitive, benign alerts generated by medical devices and clinical infrastructure. Conventional automated alert suppression approaches pose catastrophic clinical risks: if an automated system suppresses a true threat targeting a life-supporting medical device (e.g., a ventilator or infusion pump), patient safety is directly jeopardized.

The technical challenge is to design and evaluate an automated triage assistant that:
1. Effectively recognizes and suppresses high-confidence, repetitive false positives.
2. Uncompromisingly detects and preserves novel, previously unseen threat vectors.
3. Completely prevents autonomous closure of alerts targeting life-critical medical devices.
4. Generates interpretable, evidence-based reasoning for every recommendation.
5. Continuously learns from human analyst dispositions without destabilizing the core detection model.

# 5. Problem Analysis
A rigorous analysis of healthcare SOC operations reveals several systemic challenges:
1. **Asymmetric Cost of Errors:** In conventional IT, a false positive wastes time, while a false negative causes data loss. In a clinical hospital SOC, a false negative on an IoMT device can result in physical harm or death. Therefore, standard classification accuracy is an inappropriate optimization metric; minimization of False Negatives (maximizing Threat Recall) is mandatory.
2. **Telemetry Heterogeneity and Legacy Stack:** Medical devices communicate using domain-specific protocols (DICOM, HL7, proprietary UDP beacons). Operating systems are frequently embedded, unpatchable, or lack antivirus agent support.
3. **Stream Jitter and Imperfections:** Clinical telemetry traverses segmentation gateways, Wi-Fi networks, and IoT concentrators, resulting in duplicated events, clock skews, transmission delays, and out-of-order arrivals.
4. **Adversarial Mimicry:** Sophisticated threat actors intentionally craft attacks to resemble low-severity baseline noise (e.g., mimicking routine scheduled vulnerability scanners or internal maintenance reboots).

# 7. Existing System
Current hospital SOC operations rely primarily on:
1. **Static SIEM Correlation Rules:** Hardcoded threshold rules (e.g., "Alert if >10 failed logins within 60 seconds", "Alert if external IP connects to VLAN 12").
2. **Tier-1 Manual Triage:** Human analysts manually review alert queues in chronological order, pivoting across multiple consoles (EDR, Firewall, DHCP logs, CMDB) to ascertain whether an IP belongs to an authorized scanner or a clinical device.
3. **Binary Suppression Whitelists:** Static suppression lists based on IP subnets or alert IDs, which frequently become outdated and blind the SOC to compromised internal hosts.

---

# 8. Limitations of Existing System
* **Excessive Workload:** High false-positive rates (65–80%) flood the queue, creating backlogs and analyst burnout.
* **Inability to Generalize:** Static rules cannot distinguish between a benign scanner rebooting an endpoint and an adversary staging lateral movement under the guise of an admin account.
* **Fragility to Network Fluctuations:** Duplicate alert bursts from noisy sensors can swamp analyst dashboards.
* **No Adaptive Memory:** If an analyst determines forty times that a specific laboratory analyzer generates benign UDP bursts every morning at 06:00, standard SIEMs do not learn this pattern without manual rule engineering by a SIEM administrator.

---

# 9. Proposed System
The proposed **False-Positive Reduction Assistant** introduces a multi-tier defense-in-depth pipeline:
1. **Stream Integrity Processor:** Deduplicates alert storms, logs delivery latency, and chronologically re-sequences out-of-order streams.
2. **Clinical Preprocessing & Feature Engineering:** Extracts temporal contextual features, device criticality weights, and normalizes categorical headers.
3. **Machine Learning Classifier:** A balanced model that scores the probability that an incoming alert represents an actionable incident.
4. **Novelty & Anomaly Protection Layer:** An Isolation Forest algorithm that flags deviations from normal operational telemetry envelopes.
5. **Clinical Safety Guardrail:** A deterministic policy layer preventing suppression if an alert involves critical medical equipment or novel telemetry.
6. **Explainability Engine:** Translates internal feature weights and historical device statistics into plain-language evidence bullets.
7. **Human-in-the-Loop & Feedback Learning:** Captures analyst confirmations and overrides, maintaining an audit trail and supporting periodic model retraining.

# 15. Data Flow
1. **Raw Alert Reception:** Sensor telemetry records alert parameters (`source_ip`, `destination_ip`, `ports`, `event_counts`).
2. **Device Context Join:** Alert is matched with the medical asset inventory (`device_type`, `device_criticality`, `firmware`, `vlan`).
3. **Integrity Pass:** The record is checked against recent event IDs. If a duplicate is detected, it is filtered and counted. If delayed, latency is tagged.
4. **Feature Extraction:** Preprocessing converts timestamps to operational shift hours and scales numerical features.
5. **Parallel Inference:** The record passes simultaneously through the supervised classifier and the unsupervised Isolation Forest.
6. **Guardrail Decision:** The safety arbiter evaluates the output against medical device constraints.
7. **Human Triage & Feedback:** The analyst reviews the recommendation, inputs a final disposition, and logs any override justification.

# 16. Dataset Description

The generated dataset contains **5,330 records** across 43 attributes. The major fields include:

### Security Alert Fields
* `alert_id`: Unique alert identifier (e.g., `ALT-10001`).
* `event_id`: Unique underlying security event identifier (allows deduplication of duplicate alert storms).
* `timestamp`: ISO-8601 string of actual event occurrence on endpoint.
* `received_timestamp`: ISO-8601 string when the SIEM received the event (incorporates real-world latency).
* `alert_type`: Security alert classification (e.g., `Scheduled Vulnerability Scan`, `Ransomware File Encryption Activity`, `HL7 Burst Message Volume`).
* `severity`: Severity assigned by sensor (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `CRITICAL`).

* `protocol`: Network protocol (`TCP`, `UDP`, `HTTPS`, `SSH`, `DICOM`, `HL7`).
* `source_port` & `destination_port`: Port numbers (standard 443, 22, 80, alongside medical 104 [DICOM], 2575 [HL7], and malicious 4444, 1337).

* `login_attempts` & `failed_login_count`: Counts of authentication attempts.

* `rule_id`: Identifier of triggered signature rule.
* `detection_source`: Sensor origin (`Network NIDS`, `Host EDR`, `Firewall`, `Medical Device Gateway`, `SIEM Correlation`).
* `unusual_time`: Binary flag indicating activity outside normal operating hours (22:00–06:00).
* `unusual_destination`: Binary flag indicating destination IP never before contacted by this device class.

* `device_id`: Unique asset tag (e.g., `DEV-INF-102`).
* `device_type`: Clinical category (`Infusion Pump`, `Ventilator`, `Patient Monitor`, `CT Scanner`, `MRI Scanner`, `ECG Machine`, `Laboratory Analyzer`, `PACS Server`, `Nurse Station Workstation`).

* `firmware_version`: Firmware revision string.
* `device_criticality`: Clinical priority rank (`CRITICAL`, `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`).
* `device_location`: Physical hospital ward (`ICU-Pod-A`, `ED-Trauma-1`, `OR-Suite-03`, `Radiology-G02`).
* `network_segment`: Network zone (`Medical-Device-VLAN`, `Imaging-PACS-VLAN`, `Clinical-Workstation-VLAN`, `IoT-Telemetry-VLAN`).

### Endpoint Context Fields
* `endpoint_id` & `hostname`: Endpoint identifier and FQDN.
* `operating_system`: Device OS (`Embedded Linux`, `Windows 10 IoT Enterprise`, `VxWorks`, `Custom RTOS`, `Windows Server 2019`).
* `department`: Hospital division.
* `user_role`: Operator credential (`Clinical Nurse`, `Attending Physician`, `Biomedical Tech`, `System Service Account`).

### Analyst and Ground Truth Fields
* `analyst_id`: Analyst reviewer identifier (`ANL-101` to `ANL-105`).

# 17. Synthetic Data Generation
To ensure total patient privacy, HIPAA compliance, and cybersecurity safety, **no live hospital networks or real medical devices were connected**. All telemetry is generated synthetically using `src/data_generator.py`. 

Unlike naive random data generators, our generator enforces realistic clinical correlations:
* **Infusion Pumps and Ventilators** run embedded real-time operating systems (`VxWorks`, `Custom RTOS`), reside on `Medical-Device-VLAN`, do not support antivirus agents, and have high baseline false-positive rates due to periodic manufacturer telemetry beacons.
* **PACS Imaging Servers** communicate over port 104 (`DICOM`) with massive burst sizes, located on `Imaging-PACS-VLAN`.
* **Authorized Scanners** (`10.0.10.50–52`) operate during off-hours maintenance windows, triggering thousands of low-severity connection attempts across varied ports.
* **True Threats** exhibit correlated indicators: external non-routable or malicious public IPs, elevated failed login attempts, off-hours execution, and lateral SMB/SSH scanning.
* **Imperfections:** Includes 2.5% duplicate records, 7% delayed arrivals (5–45 minutes latency), 150 out-of-order records, and 3% missing values across optional fields to mimic real-world network turbulence.

---

# 18. Data Preprocessing
The preprocessing pipeline (`src/preprocessing.py`) prepares raw telemetry for machine learning without compromising auditability:
1. **Zero-Drop Policy:** Under no circumstances are security records dropped due to parsing errors or missing values. All 5,330 alerts are preserved.
2. **Missing Value Imputation:** Categorical fields (`patch_status`, `antivirus_status`, `firmware_version`) are imputed with `"Unknown"`. Numerical metrics are filled with baseline medians.
3. **Temporal Feature Extraction:** Timestamps are parsed into `hour_of_day`, `day_of_week`, `is_weekend`, and `latency_seconds` (reconciling clock skews).
4. **Ordinal Mapping:** `severity` and `device_criticality` are mapped to monotonic integer scales (1 to 4).
5. **Feature Interaction:** Generates composite indicators such as `high_failure_rate` ($\frac{\text{failed\_logins}}{\text{login\_attempts}}$) and `scanner_maintenance_combo`.
6. **Audit Record:** Decisions and imputation metrics are automatically recorded to `data/preprocessing_audit_log.json`.

# 19. Baseline Algorithm
The rule-based baseline (`src/baseline.py`) models common SOC triage heuristics:
* **Rule 1 (Low Severity Repetitive):** `IF severity == LOW AND historical_fp_rate > 0.80 -> LIKELY_FALSE_POSITIVE`
* **Rule 2 (Authentication Spikes):** `IF failed_login_count > 10 -> SUSPICIOUS`
* **Rule 3 (Critical Asset Deviation):** `IF device_criticality == CRITICAL AND unusual_destination == TRUE -> HIGH_RISK`
* **Rule 4 (Scheduled Assessment):** `IF known_scanner == TRUE AND maintenance_window == TRUE -> LIKELY_FALSE_POSITIVE`
* **Default:** `NEEDS_REVIEW`

Alerts predicted as `LIKELY_FALSE_POSITIVE` are suppressed. All other alerts are routed to manual analyst review.

# 20. Machine Learning Models
Three distinct algorithms were evaluated under identical 80/20 stratified cross-validation splits:
1. **Logistic Regression (Balanced Weights):** Linear decision boundary with $L_2$ regularization. Penalizes minority class misclassifications inversely proportional to class frequencies.
2. **Decision Tree (Max Depth = 6, Balanced):** Non-linear orthogonal partitioning.
3. **Random Forest (120 Estimators, Max Depth = 8, Balanced):** Ensemble of decorrelated decision trees using bootstrap aggregation.

---

# 21. Model Selection
In typical enterprise software, models are selected based on accuracy. In hospital security, this approach is dangerous: an accuracy of 94% that misses 5% of ransomware attacks on ventilators is unacceptable.

### Evaluation on 1,066 Test Samples (246 Actual Incidents):
* **Logistic Regression:** Threat Recall = **1.0000 (100%)**, Missed Incidents = **0**, FNR = **0.0000**, Precision = **80.13%**, F1 = **0.8897**, Accuracy = **94.28%**.
* **Decision Tree:** Threat Recall = **0.9512**, Missed Incidents = **12**, FNR = **0.0488**, Precision = **82.11%**, F1 = **0.8814**, Accuracy = **94.09%**.
* **Random Forest:** Threat Recall = **0.9837**, Missed Incidents = **4**, FNR = **0.0163**, Precision = **81.48%**, F1 = **0.8913**, Accuracy = **94.47%**.

**Selection Rationale:** **Logistic Regression** was chosen because it achieved a **100% Threat Recall** with **zero missed incidents** on the test set. Its calibrated probability outputs also integrate seamlessly with the downstream safety guardrail thresholds.

# 22. Novel Threat Detection
Traditional classifiers fail when confronted with zero-day attacks or novel malware tools that do not resemble historical training examples. To protect against this vulnerability, the system incorporates an unsupervised **Isolation Forest** model (`src/anomaly_detection.py`) trained on benign baseline telemetry.

The Isolation Forest calculates path lengths across 150 randomized isolation trees. Anomalous instances (such as an infusion pump beaconing to an unfamiliar foreign IP or utilizing a non-standard port) require few splits to isolate, yielding high anomaly scores. 
* Any alert with an anomaly score $>0.65$ or flagged as an outlier is automatically assigned an `INVESTIGATE` recommendation, completely overriding any false-positive suppression suggestion.

# 24. Human-in-the-Loop
The system operates as an **Assistant**, not an autonomous blocking authority. Analysts interact with the triage interface via four primary actions:
* `[Confirm False Positive]`: Accepts suppression recommendation and updates benign baseline.
* `[Confirm True Threat]`: Validates attack and initiates incident response playbook.
* `[Mark for Investigation]`: Flags ambiguous telemetry for deeper Tier-2 forensic analysis.
* `[Escalate]`: Immediately notifies clinical engineering and hospital incident command.

Whenever an analyst's decision contradicts the assistant's recommendation, an override record is logged with an mandatory justification category.

# 25. Analyst Feedback
Feedback is stored in `data/analyst_feedback.csv`. Rather than retraining incrementally on single noisy alerts (which induces catastrophic forgetting), the system implements a batched retraining pipeline (`src/feedback_learning.py`). 
* Retraining tests demonstrated that incorporating analyst overrides maintained Threat Recall at **1.0000** while preserving high triage precision (**80.13%**) and **zero missed incidents**.

# 26. Event Integrity
Real-world medical device telemetry is prone to transmission errors. The `EventProcessor` (`src/event_processor.py`) guarantees data integrity:
1. **Duplicate Elimination:** Computes a SHA-256 fingerprint from `source_ip`, `destination_ip`, `alert_type`, `device_id`, and a one-minute timestamp bucket. Duplicate arrivals increment an occurrence counter while emitting exactly one logical event for analysis.
2. **Delayed Event Reconciliation:** Alerts delayed by network congestion or offline device reconnects are assigned an accurate `latency_seconds` metric. If latency exceeds 180 seconds, an `is_delayed` flag is attached, ensuring delayed critical events are prioritized rather than discarded.
3. **Out-of-Order Resequencing:** Telemetry streams arriving out of temporal sequence are buffered and sorted chronologically based on true generation timestamps, preserving causal order.

# 30. Adversarial Testing
Detailed analysis of the primary adversarial test scenarios:
* **Adversarial Test A (Model Deception):** An attacker crafts a low-severity alert mimicking routine vulnerability scans against a Ventilator. The ML classifier evaluates a low threat score. # 31. Evaluation Metrics
* **Threat Recall:** $\frac{TP}{TP + FN}$ — Percentage of true threats successfully flagged for review.
* **False Negative Rate (FNR / Missed Incident Rate):** $\frac{FN}{TP + FN}$ — Percentage of true threats mistakenly suppressed. Must equal $0.00\%$.
* **Triage Precision:** $\frac{TP}{TP + FP}$ — Proportion of alerts sent to analysts that represent true threats.
* **F1 Score:** Harmonic mean of precision and recall.
* **Analyst Hours Required:** $\frac{\text{Alerts Requiring Review} \times 8 \text{ minutes}}{60 \text{ minutes/hour}}$
* **Analyst Hours Saved:** $\text{Baseline Hours} - \text{Proposed Hours}$
* **Workload Percentage Reduction:** $\frac{\text{Hours Saved}}{\text{Baseline Hours}} \times 100\%$

# 39. Ethical and Safety Considerations
